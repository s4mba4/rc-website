The future

The future: beyond farmland.

The principle that saves water on farms can do the same wherever plants need water to survive. RainCatcher is developing new applications for green spaces and forests. These applications are in development; no results are published for them yet.

7.1 · Public and private green spaces

Keeping green spaces green with less water

In hot countries, parks, gardens, sports fields, hotel and residential landscapes consume very large volumes of water, often treated or desalinated water, simply to stay green. Golf courses are among the largest consumers of all.

By holding water in the root zone of turf, shrubs and trees, the hydro-retainer can keep these spaces green with significantly less irrigation. RainCatcher is developing protocols and application methods for golf courses (fairways, greens and roughs), municipal parks and urban trees, sports pitches, and hotel and residential landscapes. For their managers, every cubic metre saved means lower costs and a smaller environmental footprint, and for cities, more water available for other uses.

7.2 · Young trees

Helping young trees survive drought

Reforestation and afforestation projects lose a large share of their young trees in the first summers after planting, simply because their roots cannot yet reach deep water. A water reserve placed in the planting hole gives each sapling a buffer during these critical months. Better survival rates mean fewer replantings, lower costs and forests that establish faster.

7.3 · Wildfires

Moist zones against wildfires

Wildfires spread through dry vegetation and dry soil. By keeping soil and vegetation moister in strategic strips, along firebreaks, at the edge of villages or around sensitive sites, the hydro-retainer could help create zones where fire slows down and is easier to fight. RainCatcher is exploring this application with forestry stakeholders.
