4.2 · The hydro-retainer

Water held where roots can reach it

At the heart of our solution is a patented hydro-retainer based on potassium polyacrylate. In contact with water, its granules absorb up to 230 times their own weight and swell to hold that water in the soil. As the soil around them dries, they release it progressively to the roots. With each irrigation or rainfall the cycle starts again.

  • Placed below the surface, in the root zone, where it is protected from sunlight and evaporation and where roots can reach it directly.
  • Active over several seasons, absorbing and releasing water through many cycles before it needs to be renewed.
  • Improves soil structure: as the granules swell and shrink, they help aerate the soil around the roots.
  • Works with the farm's existing practices: no change to irrigation equipment, fertilisation or harvesting.

With RainCatcher, the hydro-retainer holds the water at the roots and releases it as the soil dries.

4.3 · Our agronomic expertise

The right dose, depth and moment

A hydro-retainer only delivers its full value when it is used at the right dose, at the right depth and at the right moment. That is the role of our agronomists.

For each project, they build an application protocol from four sets of data:

The soil

Texture, depth, infiltration, salinity.

The climate

Rainfall, heat, evaporation.

The crop

The crop and its root system.

The water

The farm's water resources and irrigation system.

The protocol defines how much product to apply per hectare or per tree, where to place it (under the row, along the drip line, around the root system), when to apply it in the crop calendar, and how to adjust irrigation afterwards to capture the savings without stressing the crop.

Measured, not assumed

Our trials follow recognised experimental practice, with treated and untreated control plots, and soil-moisture sensors that let us measure water savings and plant response objectively.

4.4 · Our machinery

Dedicated machines, developed with French manufacturers

To place the hydro-retainer precisely, quickly and with minimal disturbance to soil and crops, RainCatcher has developed dedicated machines with French equipment manufacturers.

RCSeeder® for row crops

A tractor-mounted machine that places the hydro-retainer in a band under the future crop rows, at a calibrated depth and dose, before or during sowing. It fits into the normal sequence of field operations and covers large areas quickly.

Tree injector for orchards and palm groves

A machine with several injectors that places the hydro-retainer at depth around the root system of existing trees in a few minutes per tree, without uprooting or damaging them. For new orchards, the product is placed directly in the planting hole.

Configuration for market gardening

Precise placement along beds and drip lines, in open fields and greenhouses.

Machines can be operated by our teams, by partners trained by us, or by the farm itself.

4.5 · Crop by crop

One method, three ways of applying it

Row crops

Wheat, barley, maize, cotton, sunflower, sugar beet

For row crops, the hydro-retainer is placed in a band under each row before or at sowing with the RCSeeder®. The roots grow straight into a zone where moisture stays stable. On irrigated fields, the number and volume of irrigations can be reduced. On rain-fed fields, every rain is better used, which secures emergence and protects the crop during dry spells at key stages such as flowering and grain filling.

Tree crops

Date palms, olives, citrus, almonds, fruit trees

Trees are long-term investments, and losing them to drought costs years of production. In established orchards and palm groves, our tree injector places the hydro-retainer around the root system of each tree. In new plantations, it is placed in the planting hole, which helps young trees establish themselves during their first critical summers. The benefits last over several seasons: lower water use per tree, stronger growth and better fruit yield and quality.

Market gardening

Vegetables in open fields and greenhouses

Vegetables are very sensitive to irregular watering, which affects size, uniformity and quality. Placed along beds and drip lines, the hydro-retainer keeps moisture more even between irrigations. Growers can irrigate less often, reduce losses and harvest more uniform produce.

4.7 · Frequently asked questions

Your questions, answered

Does it work with my irrigation system?

Yes. The hydro-retainer works with drip, pivot, sprinkler and furrow irrigation, and on rain-fed land. It does not replace your irrigation system; it makes it more efficient.

How long does it stay active in the soil?

Several seasons. It absorbs and releases water through many cycles before it needs to be renewed. The renewal interval for your crop is set during the agronomic study.

Which soils benefit most?

Sandy and light soils, where water drains quickly, usually show the greatest benefit. Heavier soils also benefit, especially in hot climates. Saline conditions are assessed case by case.

Do I need to change how I farm?

No. Fertilisation, crop protection and harvesting stay the same. Only irrigation is adjusted, following our agronomists' recommendations, so that you capture the water savings.

Do I need to buy the machinery?

Not necessarily. Application can be carried out by our teams or trained partners, and machines can also be made available to the farm.

When will I see results?

From the first season. A pilot over one or two seasons, with treated and control plots, gives you measured results on your own farm before any large-scale decision.

Is it worth the investment?

That depends on your crop, your water costs and your yields, which is exactly what the agronomic study assesses. In high-value crops and where water is expensive, the investment can be recovered quickly; in the Hatay cotton demonstration it was recovered in the first season.*

*Results observed in specific trials. Every farm and every crop is assessed through a preliminary agronomic study and will achieve its own results. See our full disclaimer.
