What we do

We help farms grow more with less water.

Our job is to turn every cubic metre of irrigation and every millimetre of rain into more harvest, and to keep that harvest safe when water runs short.

3.1 · The water challenge

Water is the limit, and much of it is lost

Agriculture accounts for around 70% of freshwater withdrawals worldwide. In the Middle East and North Africa, the most water-scarce region in the world, the share is even higher. At the same time, climate change is making rainfall less predictable and heat waves more frequent, increasing the water needs of crops precisely when water is hardest to find.

On many farms, the problem is not only how much water is available but how much of it is lost. In sandy and light soils, irrigation water drains quickly below the root zone. Under a hot sun, the top layer of the soil dries within hours. Farmers compensate by irrigating more often and with larger volumes, which raises pumping costs, depletes wells and reservoirs, and still leaves crops exposed between two irrigations.

RainCatcher tackles this loss where it happens: in the soil, around the roots. By turning the root zone into a water reserve, we make every drop of water work harder for the crop.

3.2 · Five benefits for your farm

What changes in the field

1

Increase yields

Plants that have steady access to water grow better. By keeping moisture constant in the root zone, the hydro-retainer reduces the stress plants suffer between two irrigations or two rains. Roots develop more fully, nutrients are taken up more efficiently and the crop can express its full potential. In our trials, yield increases have been recorded on cereals, cotton, tree crops and vegetables.*

2

Drastically reduce water consumption

The hydro-retainer absorbs water during irrigation or rainfall and gives it back to the roots gradually, as the soil dries. Less water drains away and less evaporates, so the farm can irrigate less often and with smaller volumes. For the grower, this means lower water bills, lower energy costs for pumping, less labour and less wear on equipment. In our trials, irrigation reductions of 40 to 80% have been observed.*

3

Protect yields in severe drought

A dry year can wipe out a season's margin. When rains fail or water allocations are cut, the reserve placed at the roots acts as an insurance policy for the harvest: crops hold on longer during dry spells and recover faster afterwards. For a farm, this means steadier production and steadier income from one season to the next, and for a region, a more secure food supply.*

4

One universal solution, for irrigated and rain-fed land

Most water-saving technologies only work on irrigated land. RainCatcher works on both. On irrigated farms, it complements every system, drip, pivot, sprinkler or furrow, and reduces the water each one needs. On rain-fed land, it captures and stores rainfall that would otherwise be lost, making yields more reliable.

This is what makes our solution unique: it can give new value to land that was considered marginal or too risky to farm, and bring more of a country's rain-fed land into reliable production.

5

Cultivate far more land with the same water

Many farms depend on a fixed volume of water: a quota, a well, or a storage reservoir filled during the rainy season. When each hectare needs less water, the same volume irrigates more hectares. For farms that rely on storage reservoirs, the cultivated area can increase sharply without using a single additional cubic metre.

An example

A farm stores 100,000 m³ of water in a reservoir and irrigates 50 hectares at 2,000 m³ per hectare. If RainCatcher reduces the water needed per hectare by 40%, the same reservoir can irrigate about 83 hectares, two thirds more. With a 50% reduction, it can irrigate 100 hectares, twice the area.

Illustrative example only. Actual savings depend on crop, soil and climate, and are determined during the agronomic study.

*Results observed in specific trials. Every farm and every crop is assessed through a preliminary agronomic study and will achieve its own results. See our full disclaimer.
